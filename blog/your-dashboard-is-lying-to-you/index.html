Your dashboard is lying to you (and it's not the data's fault)

Every analyst has lived this moment: you ship a beautiful dashboard, leadership nods politely, and three weeks later two directors are arguing about whether the business is up or down — using the same chart.

The problem is almost never the data. It's that a dashboard without a decision it's meant to serve is just decoration. Before I build anything at Cox Media Group, I ask three questions.

1. What decision does this change?

If a metric can't move a budget, a headcount, or a strategy, it doesn't belong on the executive view. It belongs in the appendix. Ruthless editing is the highest-leverage design skill in analytics.

2. What's the benchmark?

A number without a reference point is trivia. Is $2.4M in revenue good? Only relative to plan, to last year, and to the cost that produced it. Every KPI on my dashboards ships with a benchmark baked in.

A metric without a benchmark is a fact. A metric with a benchmark is a decision.

3. Who owns the "so what"?

The best dashboards assign a name to every red cell. Measurement without ownership is just anxiety at scale.

Get these three right and the tool practically narrates itself — which, not coincidentally, is when people finally start trusting it.
