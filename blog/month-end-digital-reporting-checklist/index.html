The month-end digital reporting checklist for multi-channel teams

Month-end reporting across several digital channels is rarely hard because of the math. It's hard because of the coordination: a dozen platforms, a few spreadsheets, shifting definitions, and a deadline. A repeatable checklist removes most of the stress.

The day counts below are an example cadence. Adjust them to your close calendar.

Before the month closes

  • Confirm the metric definitions. Conversions, revenue, attribution window, and source of truth should be written down and unchanged from last month, or any change should be flagged.
  • Have plan and budget numbers ready. Reporting without a target is just data.
  • Verify access. A missing login on the last day of the month costs more time than any analysis does.

Business days 1–2: pull and settle

  • Let the data settle. Late conversions, delayed platform processing, and offline imports mean day-one numbers will move.
  • Pull every channel on the same date range and time zone. Search, social, programmatic, OTT, video, and email all get the same cut.
  • Reconcile spend against invoices or finance records. Spend that doesn't tie out makes every efficiency metric wrong.

Business days 2–3: QA

  • Look for missing days, duplicate rows, and currency mismatches.
  • Check for tracking breaks: a channel whose conversions dropped to zero overnight is a tag problem until proven otherwise.
  • Measure the share of unclassified traffic (missing or inconsistent UTMs) and fix the mapping.
  • Compare platform-reported conversions to your source of truth and note any shifts in the gap. (See my post on why platform conversions don't match GA4.)

Business days 3–4: calculate and interpret

  • Calculate the core KPIs: spend, impressions, clicks, CTR, CPC, CPM, CPA, ROAS, MER, and ROI (see ROAS vs MER vs ROI).
  • Compare each against plan, prior month, and prior year.
  • Write the narrative: what changed, why it changed, what it means, and what decision is needed. Assign an owner to every red flag.

Business day 5: publish and freeze

  • Distribute the report and archive a snapshot of the final numbers, so history doesn't quietly change.
  • Log any restatements and the reason for each.
  • Run a five-minute retro: what broke this month, and what gets automated before the next close?
A good month-end report isn't the one with the most charts. It's the one where no one has to ask "can we trust this?"
