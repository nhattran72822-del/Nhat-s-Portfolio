Why your ad platform conversions never match GA4 (and how to reconcile them)

Every marketing team eventually has this meeting: Google Ads reports 120 conversions, Meta reports 95, GA4 reports 70, and finance reports something else entirely. Someone asks which number is right, and the room goes quiet.

The honest answer is that each system is answering a different question. Once you see that, the mismatch stops being a crisis and becomes something you can manage.

Why the numbers disagree

1. Different attribution models. Ad platforms generally credit conversions to their own ads. GA4 looks across all channels and splits credit using its own model. When two systems are allowed to claim the same sale, the totals will not add up to reality.

2. Different lookback windows and view-through credit. Platforms apply their own click and view windows, and many count a conversion when someone saw an ad but never clicked. GA4 is typically stricter about what it counts.

3. Different timing. Many platforms report a conversion against the date of the ad interaction, while GA4 reports it on the date the conversion actually happened. Compare the two on a single day or a single month and they will not line up, especially around month-end.

4. Measurement gaps. Consent choices, ad blockers, cross-device journeys, and browser restrictions mean the tag on your site sees less than the platform's modeled data does. GA4 usually undercounts relative to the platforms for this reason.

5. Different definitions. What counts as a conversion? A purchase, a form fill, or a qualified lead? Is it counted once per user or every time? Duplicate events, time zones, and currency settings quietly widen the gap further.

A reconciliation process that works

  • Decide which system answers which question. Use platform data to optimize inside a channel, GA4 or a warehouse to compare channels, and the CRM or finance system for revenue you can bank.
  • Write the definitions down. One page: what each conversion means, the window, the counting method, and the source of truth. Most reporting arguments are definition arguments.
  • Enforce UTM governance. If traffic can't be classified, it can't be reconciled. Track the share of "unassigned" or unmapped traffic as a health metric.
  • Compare like with like. Same date basis, same window, same conversion definition, before you compare any numbers.
  • Track the gap itself. Calculate the ratio of platform-reported to source-of-truth conversions for each channel, every month. A stable ratio is normal. A ratio that suddenly shifts usually means a broken tag, a changed setting, or a tracking outage.
Don't chase a perfect match. Chase a stable, explainable gap.

What to put in front of leadership

Report one headline number from your chosen source of truth, show the platform-reported figures beside it, and add a one-line note explaining the difference. Executives don't need the numbers to be identical. They need to know which one to trust and why.
