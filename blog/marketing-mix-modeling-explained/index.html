Marketing Mix Modeling, minus the mystique

When people hear "marketing mix modeling" they picture a statistical séance. In reality, the intuition behind MMM fits on a napkin: the first dollar in a channel works harder than the last.

That single idea — the saturation curve — is why pouring your entire budget into last-click search eventually stops paying off, and why the "obviously best" channel isn't always where the next dollar should go.

The marginal-return rule

The optimal mix isn't the one where every channel has the highest average ROI. It's the one where the next dollar in every channel returns the same amount. When marginal returns are equal, you can't do better by moving money around. That's the whole game.

You can feel this yourself in the Media Mix Modeling tool on this site — set a lopsided budget, hit optimize, and watch it push dollars off the flat part of one curve onto the steep part of another.

What MMM won't tell you

  • It won't give you person-level attribution — it's a top-down view.
  • It struggles with channels that have little spend variation.
  • It needs enough history to see the curves bend.

Used honestly, though, MMM answers the one question every CMO actually asks: where does the next dollar go?
